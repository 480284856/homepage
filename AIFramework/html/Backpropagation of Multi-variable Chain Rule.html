Backpropagation of Multi-variable Chain Rule

This article explains how gradients are backpropagated when a small change in one variable affects multiple output neurons.

Let's say we have a neural network with 2 input neurons, multiple hidden layers with two neurons each, and 1 output neuron.

Now let's define the symbols of each neuron in the hidden layers. We define $a^{(n)L}_i$ as the output of the $i$-th neuron in the $L$-th hidden layer in the $n$-th batch. For example, as shown in the image above, $a^{(n)L}_1$ is the output of the first neuron in the $L$-th hidden layer in the $n$-th batch, and $a^{(n)L}_2$ is the output of the second neuron in the $L$-th hidden layer in the $n$-th batch.

Let's say we have the value matrix of each neuron in layer $M$:

$$ V_M = \begin{bmatrix} a^{(1)M}_{1} & a^{(1)M}_{2} \\ a^{(2)M}_{1} & a^{(2)M}_{2} \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ a^{(BS)M}_{1} & a^{(BS)M}_{2} \end{bmatrix} $$

In my opinion, the loss function $L$ is a function of the value matrix $V_M$:

$$ L(V_M): \mathbb{R}^{BS \times 2} \to \mathbb{R} $$

or:

$$ L(V_M) = F(a^{(1)M}_{1}, a^{(1)M}_{2}, \cdots, a^{(BS)M}_{1}, a^{(BS)M}_{2}) $$

According to the rules of multivariable differentiation, the differential of a multivariable function can be expressed as a linear combination of the differentials of all independent variables, each multiplied by the function's partial derivative with respect to that specific independent variable:

$$ dL(V_M) = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{1}} \Delta a^{(1)M}_{1} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{2}} \Delta a^{(1)M}_{2} + \cdots + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)M}_{1}} \Delta a^{(BS)M}_{1} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)M}_{2}} \Delta a^{(BS)M}_{2} $$

Now we look at the relationship between the values at layer $M$ and the values at layer $L$. Assume that between layer $L$ and layer $M$, there is no activation function, or the activation function is $y=x$.

Therefore, each neuron value in layer $M$ is a linear combination of all neuron values in layer $L$ with the weights between them:

$$ \begin{align*} \Delta a^{(1)M}_{1} &= \Delta a^{(1)L}_{1} w_{11} + \Delta a^{(1)L}_{2} w_{12} \\ \Delta a^{(1)M}_{2} &= \Delta a^{(1)L}_{1} w_{21} + \Delta a^{(1)L}_{2} w_{22} \\ \cdots \\ \Delta a^{(BS)M}_{1} &= \Delta a^{(BS)L}_{1} w_{11} + \Delta a^{(BS)L}_{2} w_{12} \\ \Delta a^{(BS)M}_{2} &= \Delta a^{(BS)L}_{1} w_{21} + \Delta a^{(BS)L}_{2} w_{22} \end{align*} $$

If we only change $\Delta a^{(1)L}_{1}$ a little bit, then only $\Delta a^{(1)M}_{1}$ and $\Delta a^{(1)M}_{2}$ will change a little bit as well. Other values, such as $\Delta a^{(2)M}_{1}$, $\Delta a^{(2)M}_{2}$, $\Delta a^{(BS)M}_{1}$, and $\Delta a^{(BS)M}_{2}$, will not change at all.

So, the differential of $L$, when only $\Delta a^{(1)L}_{1}$ changes a little bit, is:

$$ \begin{align*} \Delta L(V_M) &= \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{1}} \Delta a^{(1)M}_{1} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{2}} \Delta a^{(1)M}_{2} \\ &= \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{1}} w_{11} \Delta a^{(1)L}_{1} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{2}} w_{21} \Delta a^{(1)L}_{1} \end{align*} $$

Therefore, the derivative of $L$ with respect to $a^{(1)L}_{1}$ is:

$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)L}_{1}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{1}} w_{11} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{2}} w_{21} $$

In the same way, for other variables at layer $L$, we have:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)L}_{2}} &= \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{1}} w_{12} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(1)M}_{2}} w_{22} \\ \cdots \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)L}_{1}} &= \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)M}_{1}} w_{11} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)M}_{2}} w_{21} \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)L}_{2}} &= \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)M}_{1}} w_{12} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(BS)M}_{2}} w_{22} \end{aligned} $$

We can write them in matrix form:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(1)L}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(1)L}} \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(2)L}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(2)L}} \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(BS)L}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(BS)L}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(1)M}} W_{11} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(1)M}} W_{21} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(1)M}} W_{12} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(1)M}} W_{22} \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(2)M}} W_{11} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(2)M}} W_{21} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(2)M}} W_{12} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(2)M}} W_{22} \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(BS)M}} W_{11} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(BS)M}} W_{21} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(BS)M}} W_{12} + \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(BS)M}} W_{22} \end{bmatrix} $$

Or, more concisely, as matrix multiplication:

$$ \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(1)L}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(1)L}} \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(2)L}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(2)L}} \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(\text{BS})L}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(\text{BS})L}} \end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}^L} = \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(1)M}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(1)M}} \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(2)M}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(2)M}} \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_1^{(\text{BS})M}} & \frac{\partial L}{\partial a_2^{(\text{BS})M}} \end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}^M} \begin{bmatrix} W_{11} & W_{12} \\ W_{21} & W_{22} \end{bmatrix} $$
